# Synthetic IELTS Writing Band 8+ Essay Collection

> **Important:** These are synthetic training records created for RAG testing. The scores and comments simulate an examiner-style rubric analysis; they are not official IELTS scores and were not written or marked by an IELTS examiner.

## Sample 1 - Cause and Effect Essay

**Reported synthetic band:** 8.0

**Prompt:** In many cities, traffic congestion is becoming worse. What are the causes of this problem, and what effects does it have on people and the environment?

**Essay:**

Traffic congestion has become a defining problem in many urban areas. Although the growth of private car ownership is the most visible cause, inadequate public transport and poorly planned development also contribute substantially. The consequences extend beyond longer journeys, affecting public health, productivity and the natural environment.

The first cause is the rapid increase in the number of cars. As household incomes rise, private vehicles become more affordable and are often viewed as more convenient than buses or trains. This tendency is reinforced when public transport is infrequent, overcrowded or unavailable in outer suburbs. A second factor is urban planning that separates homes from workplaces and essential services. Consequently, large numbers of commuters travel towards the same business districts at similar times.

The immediate effect is a considerable loss of time. Workers may spend several hours travelling each day, leaving them less time for family, exercise and rest. Congestion also reduces economic efficiency because deliveries are delayed and employees arrive late or tired. More seriously, idling vehicles release pollutants into densely populated neighbourhoods. Prolonged exposure to these emissions can worsen respiratory illness, while traffic noise may contribute to stress and sleep disruption.

In conclusion, traffic jams are primarily caused by rising car ownership, weak public transport and concentrated urban development. They waste time, damage productivity and create serious health and environmental costs. Cities should therefore combine reliable mass transit with planning policies that place homes, employment and services closer together.

**Synthetic examiner-style comments:**

- **Task Response: 8.0** - Both causes and effects are fully addressed, with a clear position and relevant development.
- **Coherence and Cohesion: 8.0** - Ideas progress logically. Cohesive devices such as “consequently”, “the immediate effect” and “more seriously” are used naturally rather than mechanically.
- **Lexical Resource: 8.0** - Precise collocations include “urban development”, “economic efficiency”, “respiratory illness” and “traffic noise”.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy: 8.0** - A wide range of complex structures is controlled accurately; errors do not impede communication.

## Sample 2 - Opinion Essay

**Reported synthetic band:** 8.5

**Prompt:** Some people believe that universities should focus only on subjects that are useful for employment. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Essay:**

Universities are often urged to prioritise courses that lead directly to employment. While higher education should respond to labour-market needs, I disagree with the view that its purpose can be reduced to vocational training. A university should equip graduates with both specialised skills and the intellectual flexibility required to adapt throughout their careers.

There is a strong case for improving the connection between education and employment. Employers need graduates who can apply knowledge rather than merely recall it, and courses in engineering, healthcare and information technology can address urgent social needs. Work placements and industry projects are particularly valuable because they allow students to test theoretical ideas in realistic settings. Nevertheless, usefulness should not be defined too narrowly. Many employers value communication, critical thinking and ethical judgement, all of which can be developed through subjects such as history, languages and philosophy.

Furthermore, the employment market changes faster than university curricula can be rewritten. A degree designed for one occupation may become outdated when technology or economic conditions shift. Students who have learned how to analyse evidence, question assumptions and acquire new knowledge independently are better placed to respond to such changes. For example, a graduate trained in research and clear writing may move successfully between publishing, policy work and business communication, even if none of these roles existed in its current form when the degree began.

The most defensible approach is therefore a balanced one. Universities should offer practical experience and maintain dialogue with employers, but they should also protect academic disciplines whose benefits are broader and less immediate. This combination produces graduates who can enter work competently and continue learning when their first career path changes.

**Synthetic examiner-style comments:**

- **Task Response: 8.5** - The writer presents a consistent opinion, acknowledges the opposing view and develops a well-supported balanced argument.
- **Coherence and Cohesion: 8.5** - Paragraphing is purposeful. Referencing and substitution avoid repetitive linking phrases.
- **Lexical Resource: 8.5** - Vocabulary is varied and accurate, including “labour-market needs”, “vocational training”, “ethical judgement” and “outdated”.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy: 8.5** - Complex sentences are frequent and controlled, with natural subordination and accurate punctuation.

## Sample 3 - Discussion Essay

**Reported synthetic band:** 8.0

**Prompt:** Some people think that governments should spend money on public libraries, while others believe that digital resources make libraries unnecessary. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

**Essay:**

The expansion of digital information has led some people to question whether public libraries still deserve government funding. Digital resources are undoubtedly convenient and inexpensive, but I believe libraries remain necessary because they provide equal access, professional guidance and a shared place for learning.

Those who favour reducing funding argue that most information can now be found online. Search engines give users immediate access to books, journals and educational videos without requiring them to travel. In addition, maintaining physical buildings and printed collections is expensive. From this perspective, governments could reach more people by investing in online databases and digital literacy programmes rather than preserving traditional library services.

However, this argument assumes that everyone has a reliable internet connection, a suitable device and the skills needed to judge online information. This is not the case for low-income families, older people or residents of remote areas. Libraries help close this digital divide by providing computers, quiet study spaces and staff who can direct users towards credible sources. They also support activities that cannot be replaced easily by a website, including language classes, reading groups and assistance with job applications.

In my view, libraries should evolve rather than disappear. A modern library can lend electronic materials and teach digital skills while continuing to offer books and face-to-face support. Public investment is justified because the service benefits communities as a whole, particularly those who would otherwise be excluded from education and reliable information.

**Synthetic examiner-style comments:**

- **Task Response: 8.0** - Both positions are discussed and the writer's own view is explicit in the introduction and conclusion.
- **Coherence and Cohesion: 8.0** - The contrast between the two views is clear, with cohesive devices used accurately and sparingly.
- **Lexical Resource: 8.0** - The response uses topic-specific terms such as “digital divide”, “credible sources” and “public investment” appropriately.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy: 8.0** - Sentence structures are varied and generally error-free, including concessive and relative clauses.
